### [H2] Case Concept 
The case simulates an end-to-end consulting project. You assume the role of squads hired to solve an ambiguous business problem, using AI as an accelerator for diagnosis, analysis, automation, and recommendations. 

### [H3] Customer Context 
Vértice Retail is a digital fashion, beauty, and lifestyle brand targeting young adults. The company grew rapidly via e-commerce, social networks, influencers, and marketplaces, but profitability has begun to decline. 
The board realizes that reports are manual, data is fragmented, and decisions still rely heavily on gut feeling. The company wants to use data and AI to professionalize its management and improve results in the short term. 
E-commerce: Fast growth, pressed margin, fragmented data, low productivity. 

### [H3] Central Challenge 
Respond to the client's board (CEO, CFO, CMO, and COO): How can we use data and AI to improve profitability, operational efficiency, and the quality of decision-making over the next 90 days?

### [H2] Potential Business Problems to Investigate 
The items below are examples of hypotheses and lines of investigation to inspire the diagnosis. They are not pre-packaged conclusions — explore the data, validate or refute hypotheses, and identify other relevant causes. 
How to use this section: think of the cards as starting points for the diagnosis. Formulate questions, test evidence, and discover which problems actually explain the client's drop in profitability and efficiency. Each card includes an objective indicating what the investigation should produce as an actionable result for the board. 

#### Margin (Hypothesis Example) 
##### Growth might be coming with poorer margin quality 
Aggressive discounts, shipping costs, returns, or shifts in the product mix could be reducing the contribution margin generated per order. 
Investigate: where is the margin deteriorating — by product, category, channel, or period? 
Objective: Quantify margin loss by dimension and recommend which categories, channels, or products to prioritize for recovery. 

#### Marketing (Hypothesis Example) 
##### Not all acquisition growth generates equally valuable customers 
Some channels may deliver conversion or volume, but with high CAC, low retention, or lower margin throughout the journey. 
Investigate: which channels bring sustainable growth — and which only increase volume? 
Objective: Classify channels by acquisition quality and recommend marketing budget reallocation based on long-term profitability. 

#### Operations (Hypothesis Example) 
##### Operational failures might be destroying value after the sale 
Stockouts, delays, returns, lead times, or concentration of issues in specific SKUs can lead to revenue loss and additional costs. 
Investigate: which operational events have the greatest financial impact and on which products? 
Objective: Size the financial cost of each type of operational failure and prioritize improvements with the highest potential for margin recovery. 

#### Customer Service (Hypothesis Example) 
##### Customer support may be concentrating symptoms of recurring issues 
Ticket volume may reveal patterns of delays, product issues, billing, or post-purchase experiences — as well as opportunities for automated screening. 
Investigate: which topics repeat most frequently, which require priority, and which could be automated? 
Objective: Map the root causes of the most frequent tickets and estimate the productivity gain/cost reduction from automating screening. 

#### Customer (Hypothesis Example) 
##### Growth may mask important differences between customer segments 
Customers with similar purchase behaviors may show very different margins, recurrence, average ticket, and acquisition costs. 
Investigate: which profiles concentrate value, churn risk, or retention potential? 
Objective: Segment the customer base and identify which segments should receive retention, upsell, or budget reduction actions. 

#### Management & Productivity (Hypothesis Example) 
##### Part of the inefficiency may lie in how information turns into decisions 
Manual reports, recurring analyses, and fragmented data can delay decisions and consume team time that could be spent on higher-value problems. 
Investigate: where do information bottlenecks, rework, or real opportunities for AI usage exist? 
Objective: Identify processes with the most informational rework and estimate recoverable hours/costs using automation and applied AI.

### [H2] Possible Solution Modules with AI 
You can choose one or more modules or develop your own solution, provided that the proposal addresses a real client pain point and has a clear connection to financial, operational, or management impact. 

#### Module A: Funnel Analysis and Channel Efficiency 
An agent that builds aggregated analyses by channel and period, identifies conversion bottlenecks, and documents attribution limits between marketing and sales. 
* Maps where the funnel loses volume and margin by channel. 
* Compares CAC, ROAS, and average margin across acquisition channels. 
* Identifies channels that generate volume but attract low-profitability customers. 
Decision enabled for the board: Which acquisition channels should receive more or less investment to maximize margin (not just volume). 
Example output: "Influencer channel: highest volume of conversions, but CAC is 2.4x above average and average margin is negative in 18% of orders." 

#### Module B: Intelligent Support Ticket Classifier 
AI to classify tickets by topic, urgency, sentiment, churn risk, and recommended action. 
* Reduces screening time. 
* Identifies automatable requests. 
* Prioritizes critical customers. 
Decision enabled for the board: Where to automate customer support and which customers require immediate intervention to prevent churn. 
Example: Topic: delivery delay · Sentiment: negative · Priority: high · Action: send tracking and compensation. 

#### Module C: Margin Prioritization Engine 
A simple model to rank initiatives based on estimated impact, effort, risk, and capture speed. 
* Compares commercial and operational opportunities. 
* Helps the board decide where to act first. 
* Separates quick wins from structural initiatives. 
Decision enabled for the board: Which sequence of actions to adopt over the next 90 days to capture the highest return with the least effort. 
Example: "Reducing discounts on high-demand SKUs has high impact, low effort, and captures value in under 30 days." 

#### Module D: Weekly Automated Report Generator 
Automation that transforms data and analyses into an executive memo containing facts, causes, risks, and next steps. 
* Standardizes management communication. 
* Reduces manual report compiling. 
* Connects KPI, explanation, and decision. 
Decision enabled for the board: Monitor KPI trends weekly without relying on manual compilation, allowing focus on corrective actions. 
Example: "Revenue grew 8%, but margin fell by 3 p.p. The main factor was increased discounting in the Fashion category."

### [H2] Additional Observations 
You have the freedom to define the approach, architecture, and toolset that best support the solution. The goal of the case is for you to build the best possible answer to the business problem — without being restricted to a single platform. 

### [H3] Stack Freedom 
EloAgents can be used as part of the solution, but it is not mandatory or exclusive. You can use, combine, or develop solutions with any AI, automation, analytics, or development tools you deem appropriate — as long as the final delivery meets the standards and objectives of the case. Choose the technology based on the problem, not the problem based on the technology. 

### [H3] Traceability of Process Artifacts (Mandatory Delivery) 
In addition to the Final Presentation, key process artifacts produced by AI agents throughout each stage must be attached to the squad's submission. The intention is to demonstrate the depth and robustness of the work — not just the final answer, but also the evidence, validations, and intermediate results supporting the conclusions. 
These process artifacts must be provided in Markdown (.md), PDF (.pdf), or HTML (.html), depending on the most appropriate format for each output. 
* Final reports produced by agents, such as management reports or consolidated executive memos. 
* Intermediate results of validation and sanity checks performed by reviewer, critic, or specialist agents. 
* Deterministic calculations executed by agents via code or sandboxes, including assumptions, results, and evidence. 
* Partial analyses and specialized outputs, such as segmentations, classifications, diagnoses, and prioritizations. 
* Other relevant evidence from the agentic process that allows an understanding of how the results were constructed. 
The Final Presentation does not replace the delivery of the process artifacts that support the work. Make the process traceable — it shows consulting maturity and lends credibility to your recommendations.

### [H2] Glossary of Case Terms 
To avoid ambiguity, the terms below are used with fixed meanings throughout the document. 
* **Case:** The evaluative exercise of the Bootcamp — the business problem you must solve as a squad. 
* **Project:** The consulting simulation for the client Vértice Retail. The case simulates a real project. 
* **Squad:** The team of participants working together to solve the case. Equivalent to the consulting team in the simulated project. 
* **Board of Directors:** The client's executive audience (CEO, CFO, CMO, and COO of Vértice Retail) to whom the squad presents recommendations. 
* **Final Presentation:** The main deliverable: the executive presentation the squad makes to the client's board, following the outline in Section 8. 
* **Demonstration:** The part of the Final Presentation where the squad shows the prototype or AI solution in action (Slide 7 of the outline). 
* **Deliverables:** The final evaluated submissions of the case: diagnosis, analysis, dashboard, prototype, business case, roadmap, governance, and Final Presentation. 
* **Process Artifacts:** Intermediate outputs produced by AI agents throughout the project (reports, calculations, classifications, validations). Must be delivered as evidence of traceability.
